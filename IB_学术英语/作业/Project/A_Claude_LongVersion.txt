## Results: Mouse Embryos Exhibit Large Tension and Compression Oscillations

In mouse embryos, we observed substantial oscillatory forces during blastocyst development using RGD-alginate soft elastic microgels (Young's modulus E = 3.1 kPa, diameter ~25 μm). Due to embryo size constraints (<100 μm), these smaller probes were injected at the 16-cell morula stage (E3.0) without magnetic crosses. Our measurements revealed large normal traction force oscillations (~1000 Pa) that significantly dominated over shear forces (~100 Pa), indicating that developing mouse embryos primarily generate alternating pushing and pulling forces. Microgels at different spatial positions within the same embryo exhibited similar oscillation patterns with position-dependent magnitude variations. Notably, embryos eventually extruded these biocompatible probes (27-31 hours post-injection), suggesting a protective cellular response similar to aneuploid cell removal mechanisms observed in human embryos. These findings contrast with our zebrafish embryo observations, indicating species-specific mechanical strategies driving vertebrate embryogenesis.

## Materials and Methods

We fabricated biocompatible ferromagnetic microrobots by creating Co-Pt (51%:49%) microcrosses through multi-layer sputtering, photolithography, electrodeposition, and thermal annealing at 675°C. The microcrosses were designed asymmetrically (21 μm × 31 μm) with small protrusions to minimize aggregation while optimizing microfluidic encapsulation. The microrobot probes were generated using a microfluidic T-junction device where polydisperse droplets containing individual microcrosses in 4.5 wt% 8-arm PEG-DBCO (20 kDa) with RGD peptides and fluorescent nanoparticles were cross-linked with 15 wt% PEG 8-NBA via picoinjection. Brief UV exposure (5 mW/cm², 6 min) photodegraded the stiff PEG microgels to enable force measurements, while an oscillatory magnetic field allowed stiffness assessment via microcross rotation. For mouse embryo studies, we used smaller RGD-alginate microgels (25 μm diameter, E = 3.1 kPa) without magnetic components due to size constraints. The embedded fluorescent nanoparticles enabled precise tracking of microgel deformations for quantifying traction forces throughout embryonic development.
