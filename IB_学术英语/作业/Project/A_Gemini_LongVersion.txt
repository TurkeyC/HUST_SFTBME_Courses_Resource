**RESULTS: Mouse embryos exhibit large tension and compression oscillations**

Investigation into the mechanical forces in developing mouse embryos revealed dominant normal traction forces (tension and compression) compared to significantly smaller shear traction forces. Experiments using injected soft elastic microgels demonstrated large oscillatory normal traction forces during the blastocyst stage, indicative of the embryo generating substantial pulling and pushing forces. These oscillations, with amplitudes around $1000 \text{ Pa}$ for normal forces and approximately $100 \text{ Pa}$ for shear forces, were consistently observed and are notably distinct from those seen in zebrafish embryos. The embryo's active extrusion of the microgels further underscored its dynamic mechanical behavior. These findings highlight the critical role of cell-generated traction forces in early mouse embryogenesis and suggest potential species-specific differences in the utilization of normal versus shear forces to drive developmental processes.

**MATERIALS AND METHODS**

The study employed biocompatible ferromagnetic cobalt-platinum (Co-Pt) microcrosses as key components of a magnetic microrobot probe designed to quantify mechanical properties in biological samples. The fabrication of these microcrosses involved a multi-step process including sputtering, photolithography, electrodeposition of Co-Pt, and annealing to achieve desired magnetic properties. The microcrosses were then integrated into photodegradable poly(ethylene glycol) (PEG) microgels using a microfluidic T junction and picoinjection technique. This method allowed for the high-throughput generation of microdroplets containing fluorescent nanoparticles and the controlled introduction of cross-linkers, resulting in the formation of the microrobot probes. The elemental composition and magnetic characteristics of the Co-Pt microcrosses were confirmed using EDS and vibrating sample magnetometry, respectively, ensuring the probes' suitability for force and stiffness quantification.
