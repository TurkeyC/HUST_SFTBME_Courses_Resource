**From Track to Wilderness: The Difference Between High School and University**
— *Life is a wilderness, not a track* (from *Prometheus*)

Moving from high school to university is a truly important shift in life’s journey. This change brings deep and noticeable differences – in how we learn, what goals we set for ourselves, and What the essence of education is.

The most obvious change we'll notice is in learning itself. In high school, learning felt like something *done to* us. The schedule was fixed; we followed a plan, always moving toward a certain goal, much like a train on a track. Everything was arranged. But university is different. There are fewer classes, and more time for us to study on our own – reading, experimenting, writing. Teachers become guides, encouraging us to ask questions and dig deeper.  We have more choices now. We're no longer on that secure track; we’re standing in a vast wilderness, and the path ahead is one we must forge ourselves.

The *purpose* of learning also changes – from singular to multiple. In high school, there was only one clear goal: the entrance exam. It was intense pressure, but the direction was straightforward. At university, though, we learn for ourselves. Some aim for great discoveries in their field; others want to start businesses, or explore who they truly are.  Even temporary confusion and disappointment are allowed. The drive to learn at university comes from within: a curiosity about knowledge, a longing for our dreams, a desire for the future. No one has the right to decide what that future will be – because in this wilderness of university life, we define it ourselves.

Li Peigen, a former president of HUST, often said that university education is about developing an "engineering mindset," not just memorizing facts. In high school, we learned to solve problems with clear instructions; textbooks always gave you everything you needed. But real-world problems are often unclear and messy. Professor Li emphasized in his “Introduction to Engineering” class that university education addresses these complex issues, requiring both a problem-solving approach and an awareness of the challenges. "We aren't trained to *know* the answers," he would say, "but to know *how* to find them."  A university education teaches us to break down problems, experiment with solutions, and improve upon our approaches. When we stand at the intersection of technology and society, we learn to redefine what a problem even is.

If we always travel on a set track, we’ll never have the chance to tackle the unique challenges that only exist in the wilderness. A life without uncertainty would be dull and unfulfilling.

Going from high school to university is like stepping out of a train and into a wild landscape. High school offers a prepared track – clear direction, defined boundaries, and a sense of safety. University provides a free wilderness – many directions, no fixed path, and a mix of risk and opportunity.  The "wilderness" nature of university acts as a testing ground. It encourages students to move from seeking “perfect answers” to asking their own questions; from passively accepting instructions to actively shaping themselves. Through trial and error, we gradually discover our true selves and grow stronger.
